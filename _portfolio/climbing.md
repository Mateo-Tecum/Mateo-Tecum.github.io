# Rock Climbing

![Climbing](/assets/images/climbing/climbing_hero.jpg)

Rock climbing has become one of my favorite ways to spend my time outside of school and engineering. What started with climbing indoors has gradually turned into traveling to climb, learning new styles, working at a climbing wall, and spending as much time outdoors as I can.

Over time, I progressed from **bouldering to top rope and eventually lead climbing**, with a lot of my training focused on becoming comfortable and prepared enough to climb outdoors.

I've now had the opportunity to climb in several places across the United States and in South Africa, each of which has been a completely different experience.

---

## Getting Into Climbing

I started primarily with **indoor bouldering**, where I first learned the fundamentals of climbing movement, footwork, and route reading.

From there, I transitioned into **top-rope climbing**, which let me spend more time on longer routes and develop endurance beyond shorter boulder problems.

![Indoor Climbing](/assets/images/climbing/climbing_indoor_01.jpg)

As I became more comfortable on ropes, I started learning to **lead climb**. A big reason I wanted to learn lead was to eventually move outside, where being comfortable clipping, managing the rope, falling, and belaying becomes much more important.

![Lead Climbing](/assets/images/climbing/climbing_lead_01.jpg)

A lot of my climbing for a while became focused on preparing for outdoor sport climbing rather than only pushing grades indoors.

---

## Climbing Outdoors

Outdoor climbing became one of my favorite parts of the sport.

Compared with climbing in a gym, outdoor routes require much more route finding. Holds are not marked by color, movements are less obvious, and every area has its own style of climbing.

I've been fortunate enough to climb in several areas around the U.S. as well as South Africa.

# Night Climbing

![Night Climbing](/assets/images/climbing/night_climbing_01.jpg)

One of the more unique experiences I've had has been **climbing outdoors at night**.

Climbing with headlamps completely changes the experience. Instead of seeing an entire wall, you're often only seeing the section of rock directly around you.

![Night Climbing Route](/assets/images/climbing/night_climbing_02.jpg)

It is a very different atmosphere from a normal climbing day and has become one of my favorite climbing memories.

# Working at a Climbing Wall

![Climbing Wall](/assets/images/climbing/climbing_work_01.jpg)

Climbing also became part of my work when I worked as a **Rock Climbing Supervisor**.

Working at a climbing wall gave me a different perspective on the sport. Instead of only thinking about my own climbing, I was responsible for helping other people learn and climb safely.

My work included:

- Supervising the climbing and bouldering areas
- Teaching climbers how to belay
- Helping new climbers learn basic climbing techniques
- Teaching climbing lessons to kids
- Monitoring climbers and belayers for safe technique
- Performing equipment and facility checks
- Helping maintain the climbing wall
- Setting and modifying climbing routes
- Helping create an enjoyable environment for both new and experienced climbers

![Teaching Climbing](/assets/images/climbing/climbing_work_02.jpg)

Teaching kids was especially fun because it required explaining climbing in a completely different way than I would with an experienced climber.

Sometimes the goal was improving technique, and other times it was simply helping someone become comfortable enough to trust the rope and get higher than they had before.

## Route Setting

![Route Setting](/assets/images/climbing/route_setting_01.jpg)

Working at the wall also gave me the opportunity to help **set climbing routes**.

Route setting made me look at climbing from the opposite perspective. Instead of trying to figure out the intended movement, I was the one deciding where holds should go and what movement they should create.

It became one of my favorite parts of working at the wall.

# What I'm Working On Now

Recently, I've shifted more of my attention back toward **bouldering**.

After spending a lot of time improving my rope climbing and learning to lead, I wanted to put more time into shorter and more powerful movements.

Bouldering gives me the opportunity to repeatedly work on individual moves and try techniques that I would be less likely to experiment with while several meters above my last bolt.

![Current Bouldering](/assets/images/climbing/bouldering_01.jpg)

I'm still interested in continuing to improve my sport climbing, but I like moving between the different styles rather than focusing entirely on one.

---

## Crack Climbing

![Crack Climbing](/assets/images/climbing/crack_climbing_01.jpg)

One style that I've especially enjoyed whenever I've had the opportunity to try it is **crack climbing**.

Cracks require completely different techniques from most gym climbing, including hand jams, finger jams, and using the crack itself instead of relying on traditional holds.

Unfortunately, good crack climbing is difficult to find indoors, so most of my experience with it has come from the limited opportunities I've had outside or on the occasional indoor crack.

It is something I'd like to spend significantly more time learning in the future.
